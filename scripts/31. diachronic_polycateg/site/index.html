Distributional POS overlap · UD_French · 1984–2018

How grammatical categories blur, and how words drift across them over time.

University of Paris Nanterre board minutes, parsed across 35 years. We don't ask which tag a word has; we ask how its syntactic company changes, and which words wander between categories as the decades pass.

From a parsed sentence to a feature matrix

Every sentence comes parsed: each token carries a part of speech, morphological features, and dependency links. We read a word's behaviour from the company it keeps — its parent, the previous node, the following node, and its children. The word itself (X) is deliberately left out, so a word never describes itself.

For each lexical unit — a (lemma, POS) pair — we count how often each contextual feature appears, as a percentage of that unit's occurrences. The row for histoire, NOUN below reads: its parent is a verb 37% of the time, that parent is singular 77% of the time, and it has an adjectival-modifier child 7% of the time.

Each unit is now a point in feature space — a vector describing the shape of its distribution, independent of how often it occurs.

The diachronic twist

To track change over time, we split each word by period. The unit is no longer just histoire, NOUN — it becomes histoire, 1984, histoire, 1985, histoire, 1986, and so on, each with the same context features computed from that year alone.

lexical unit parent=VERB parent.number=Sing child.rel=amod …
histoire, 198441%74%9%…
histoire, 198538%77%6%…
histoire, 198635%79%8%…
……………

Now a word is a small cloud of yearly points. A word whose syntax stayed put is a tight cluster; a word that changed leaves a stretched or directed cloud. That dispersion is what we measure.

Clean the noisy annotations

Automatic parsing mistags. We drop any (lemma, POS) unit that is below 10% of that lemma's total occurrences — so a handful of tokens where Mme was tagged VERB don't masquerade as a separate word — and merge accent variants like université / universite. Symbols, punctuation, and the catch-all X tag are removed.

Measure the cloud

Displacement is how far the cloud's centre travelled from its early years to its late years. Jitter is how much it wobbles year to year. Path length is the total distance walked through time, and directness (displacement ÷ path length) tells a steady march from aimless wandering. The headline score is displacement relative to jitter, weighted by how many years back it up.

Read which features moved

For a word that travelled, we list the context features that changed most — and a 2-D map of its yearly points. A clear gradient from old to recent years means a real drift; a shapeless scatter means noise.

Confirm against the raw text

The distributional method proposes candidates. We confirm each one by counting its construction directly in the corpus — the share of a word's uses that sit in a given pattern, and how dense that pattern is per 1000 tokens.
